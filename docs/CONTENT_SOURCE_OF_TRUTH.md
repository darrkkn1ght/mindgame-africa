### 1.2 Absolute prohibitions

Drawn from Original Brief §21 and Second Pass §21. Every one of these is a hard fail:

- No fabricated clients, projects, testimonials, outcomes, faculty appointments, affiliations, accreditations, publications or programmes.
- No implication that planned laboratories, certificates, practitioner directories or research centres already exist.
- **No SportLead Africa identity.** MindGame Africa is not a sport governance, facility, event-management or infrastructure consultancy. Nothing about facilities, governance reviews, competition management or sport-project delivery belongs here.
- **Not a personal brand.** Leadership establishes credibility; MindGame Africa must remain the dominant identity. Do not reproduce the tone, sales language or individual-consulting emphasis of the founder's personal website.
- **Not reducible to sport psychology.** Psychology is a current strength; the institution is deliberately multidisciplinary.
- **No careless certificate language.** Where there is no external accreditation, describe awards only as **certificate of completion**, **certificate of participation** or **professional development programme**. Never design or word anything to imply statutory accreditation.
- **No conflation of performance support with clinical mental-health treatment.** Represent scope and referral boundaries responsibly.
- No motivational quotations or generic inspiration language used as a substitute for substance.
- No African visual motifs as decoration — no maps, flags, wildlife or stereotypical cultural motifs inserted to prove the institution is African.
- No overuse of brand colours. The site stays disciplined and readable.
- **No text-heavy pages.** See §19.
- No "Coming soon." See §18.

### 1.3 Brand and visual base

Deep navy carries intellectual and institutional weight. Green supports research, growth and active states. Yellow/gold and orange/red are selective accents for emphasis, data points, section markers, buttons and highlights. Off-white/cream softens reading surfaces so the site does not feel overly dark.

The language should feel African, contemporary, scientific, human and performance-oriented. Premium and clean without becoming sterile. Restrained motion only — subtle transitions and scientific visual cues are fine; decorative animation must never compete with content.

Tone: precise and restrained. The Second Pass §1 sets the standard — *"credible before it feels impressive."*

## 17. Photographic and visual language

### 17.1 Subject briefs

| Subject | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Athlete assessment** | Assessment as an active process — observation, structured conversation, performance testing, questionnaires where appropriate, reviewing information, giving feedback, interpreting findings with an athlete. Avoid someone sitting beside equipment simply because the equipment looks scientific. |
| **Practitioner and athlete interaction** | Professional interaction in performance settings: listening, questioning, explanation, reflection, preparation, collaboration. Must not look like counselling stock photography or a therapy clinic. |
| **Researchers at work** | Research as inquiry and disciplined work: data collection, field observation, coding, reviewing literature, discussing methods, analysing information, recording results, working with participants, presenting findings. No white coats used to look scientific. |
| **Performance data** | Screens, dashboards, notes, video, measurement tools and analysis used carefully — show people *interpreting* information. Data must appear connected to a real performance question, not be decorative graphs. |
| **Coaching environments** | Coaches giving feedback, observing, demonstrating, discussing decisions, reviewing performance, communicating with athletes. The coach as part of the performance environment, not just shouting from the sideline. |
| **University teaching and professional learning** | Teaching as active engagement: discussion, demonstration, questions, group work, explanation, applied examples. Avoid empty lecture halls and someone pointing at a slide. |
| **Team sessions** | Structured team conversations, workshops, small group interaction, review meetings, planning, reflection, performance preparation. Something meaningful is being worked through. |
| **Training and competition** | Moments with analytical or performance meaning — observing from the sideline, recording notes, reviewing an action, preparing athletes, monitoring behaviour, discussing a competition plan, analysing mistakes, reviewing after an event. |
| **Observation and analysis** | Video review, match observation, coding, note taking, performance footage, practitioner discussion, and the movement from observation to interpretation. Important because it visually connects science and professional practice. |
| **Multidisciplinary collaboration** | Where genuine, different specialists working around the same performance question. **Do not stage a group to imply MindGame Africa currently employs disciplines or departments that do not exist.** |
